What decides these cases

How immigration bond works

The immigration judge decides — not ICE

ICE can set an initial amount or refuse release entirely — but that isn't the last word. An immigration judge can review it at a bond redetermination hearing. That's why the lawyer's first move is getting on record (G-28) and requesting that hearing as fast as possible.

What the judge weighs

Family ties, a stable address, work history, community roots, any record, and whether a sponsor with lawful status will take responsibility. Families who show up with letters and documents win bonds; families who show up with only worry usually don't.

What bond usually costs

The legal minimum is $1,500; judges commonly set several thousand depending on the case. It's paid in full to ICE by someone with lawful status and refunded at the end of the case when your loved one attends every hearing. The lawyer's job is the lowest realistic number — and a yes.

Common questions

Questions we answer every week

How much does the lawyer cost and how can I pay?

Opening the case takes a $2,500 retainer, which covers the first stage: locating your loved one, getting on record with the G-28, and preparing the bond request. We accept credit and debit cards, and you don't need immigration status to hire us. The bond the judge sets is separate money paid to ICE.

Who can pay the bond to ICE?

The bond is paid in full at an ICE bond office, and the person paying must show they're lawfully present in the U.S. If you don't have status yourself, line up a relative or friend who does — it's the detail most families discover too late. The money is refunded to that same person at the end of the case.

Is the bond refunded?

Yes — at the end of the case, if your loved one attends every hearing and meets the conditions, ICE refunds the bond to the person who paid it. The refund process takes time, but the money isn't lost by losing the case: it's lost by missing court.

What if they have a criminal record?

It depends on the exact record: some history triggers mandatory detention with no bond at all, and we analyze that immediately — it's the first thing a family needs to know before spending a dollar. Many minor or old records don't bar bond, but they have to be presented to the judge properly, not hidden.

How fast can the bond hearing happen?

Bond can be requested at the first hearing or by written motion, and courts often schedule the hearing within days to a few weeks — it varies by court. What the family controls is readiness: the sponsor, the address proof, and the letters. That's why we start building the package on day one.
