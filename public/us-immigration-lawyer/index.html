What decides these cases

The route you pick first decides everything after it

Canadians have shortcuts — if you use them

Canadian citizens can get TN and L-1 status adjudicated the same day at the border, skip visa stamps most nationalities need, and apply for E-2 visas through the consulate in Toronto. A strategy that ignores these advantages wastes the strongest passport in the U.S. immigration system.

The wrong first filing follows you

A border refusal, a denied petition, or an inconsistent answer becomes part of your permanent record and surfaces at every future application. The cheapest legal advice you'll ever buy is the advice you get before the first filing.

Temporary status and green card goals can conflict

TN and E-2 require non-immigrant intent; filing for permanent residence at the wrong moment can strand you outside the country. If a green card is the real goal, the sequencing — which status first, when to file, how to travel — is the whole game.

Common questions

Questions we answer every week

What's the fastest way for a Canadian to work in the U.S.?

For most professionals with a job offer, TN status — Canadian citizens can apply at a port of entry or pre-clearance airport and receive a decision the same day, no petition or lottery required. If your occupation isn't on the USMCA list, O-1, H-1B, or L-1 are the usual alternatives, each with different timelines. The consultation identifies which door your credentials actually open.

Do Canadians need a visa to visit or work in the U.S.?

For visits, no visa is required for most Canadians — but visiting is not working. Any productive work for a U.S. entity requires proper status (TN, L-1, E-2, O-1, H-1B, and so on), and 'working remotely while visiting' is a gray zone that has caught many people at the border. If you're doing anything beyond tourism or meetings, get the status question answered before you cross.

Can I just move to the U.S. if I buy a business there?

Buying a business doesn't itself grant status — but it can qualify you for an E-2 treaty investor visa if the investment is substantial, at risk, and you'll actively run the company. The E-2 is renewable indefinitely but is not a green card. This is the firm's core specialty; see the eligibility review for the specifics.

How do Canadians get a U.S. green card?

The main routes: marriage or close family, employer sponsorship (often after TN or L-1 time), self-petitions for high-achievers (EB-2 NIW, EB-1), and the EB-5 investment program. Canada has no country backlog, so most categories are 'current' — the route choice, not the queue, usually decides your timeline.

Can I apply for a green card while on TN status?

Yes, but carefully. TN is a non-immigrant-intent status, so a pending green card case can cause problems at the border if the timing and travel aren't managed. The standard play is sequencing — moving to a dual-intent status like H-1B or L-1, or timing the filing and travel windows correctly. This is exactly the kind of trap the strategy consultation exists to prevent.

Do I need a U.S. lawyer or a Canadian one?

U.S. immigration is exclusively U.S. federal law, so you need a U.S.-licensed immigration attorney — where in the U.S. they sit doesn't matter, and everything can be handled remotely from Canada. Beware of unlicensed 'consultants': only a licensed attorney (or accredited representative) can lawfully represent you before U.S. immigration agencies.
