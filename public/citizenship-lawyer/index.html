What decides these cases

The N-400 looks like a form — it's actually a full review of your immigration life

The application re-opens your entire file

USCIS reviews how you got your green card, every trip you've taken, your taxes, and your record. Filing with an unresolved issue doesn't just risk denial — in the worst cases it can put the green card itself in jeopardy. Know before you file.

Continuous residence trips up frequent travelers

Trips over six months presume a break in residence; trips over a year usually break it outright. Snowbirds, cross-border families, and international businesspeople need the travel history analyzed before filing, not explained after an accusation.

"Good moral character" covers more than crimes

Unfiled taxes, unpaid child support, misstatements on old applications, even certain traffic patterns can be raised. Most issues are solvable — with disclosure handled correctly and documentation prepared in advance.

Common questions

Questions we answer every week

When can I apply for U.S. citizenship?

Five years after becoming a permanent resident — or three years if you got your green card through marriage and are still married to and living with your U.S. citizen spouse. You can file up to 90 days before the anniversary. Physical presence, continuous residence, and state residency requirements all apply; we confirm your exact earliest filing date.

How long does naturalization take?

Most field offices currently run several months to around a year from filing to oath, varying significantly by city. Your local office's queue — not the form — sets the pace. We give you the current estimate for your field office at the consultation.

Will an old arrest or dismissed charge hurt my application?

It depends on the offense, the outcome, and the timing — but hiding it is the one guaranteed mistake, since USCIS runs full background checks. Many records need disclosure with documentation and cause no denial; some require legal strategy first; a few mean you should not file until resolved. This is exactly what the pre-filing review is for.

I've spent long periods outside the U.S. — am I still eligible?

Trips under six months are generally fine. Six-to-twelve-month trips create a presumption you broke continuous residence, which can be rebutted with ties like a home, job, and taxes. Trips over a year typically restart part of your clock. We analyze your actual travel record and tell you your real eligibility date.

What's on the citizenship test?

An English reading, writing, and speaking assessment, plus a civics test: the officer asks up to 10 questions from the official list of 100, and you must answer 6 correctly. Applicants over certain age/residence thresholds qualify for exemptions or native-language testing. We provide study materials and mock-interview preparation.

Can my N-400 denial be fixed?

Usually, yes. Options include a hearing request (N-336), refiling once the underlying issue is cured, or addressing the specific ground — unpaid taxes, insufficient residence, a documentation gap. A denial does not take away your green card in most cases, but repeated uncounseled filings waste fees and build a bad record. Have the denial reviewed before acting.
