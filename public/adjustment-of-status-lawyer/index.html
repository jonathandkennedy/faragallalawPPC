What decides these cases

Adjustment cases stall on details that were knowable before filing

Eligibility lives in your entry and status history

Adjusting inside the U.S. generally requires a lawful entry, and — outside the immediate-relative rules — maintained status. Old overstays, unauthorized work, Visa Waiver entries, and prior filings each change the analysis. Twenty minutes of history review decides whether you can adjust here or must process abroad — where a wrong guess can trigger a 3- or 10-year bar.

It's five-plus filings moving as one

The I-485 travels with the petition (I-130 or I-140), the work permit (I-765), the travel document (I-131), the financial sponsorship (I-864), and — under current rules — the medical exam (I-693), which must be submitted with the filing or risk rejection. A missing signature or an outdated fee on any one of them can bounce the entire package.

Mid-case moves are where approvals die

Leave the country without advance parole and most pending adjustments are considered abandoned (H-1B and L-1 holders get a narrow exception). Change jobs too early in an employment case and portability rules decide your fate. The case has rules of the road for the entire year-plus it's pending — we brief you on all of them at filing.

Qualify yourself in 30 seconds

Does adjustment of status fit your case?

This service is likely a fit if…

  • You're in the U.S. and married to (or petitioned by) a U.S. citizen or resident
  • Your employer's I-140 is approved or ready and your priority date is current
  • You entered on a K-1, married within 90 days, and need the green card stage
  • You were granted asylum at least one year ago
  • You entered legally but overstayed — immediate relatives of citizens usually can still adjust

It's probably not the right fit if…

  • You're outside the U.S. — that's consular processing, which we also handle end to end
  • You entered without inspection and have no 245(i) or parole basis — the path likely runs through a waiver and a consulate; we'll map it honestly
  • Your priority date isn't current yet — filing now would be rejected; we'll calendar the moment it opens

Common questions

Questions we answer every week

What's the difference between adjustment of status and consular processing?

Adjustment (I-485) gets you the green card without leaving the U.S. — you stay, and can get a work permit and travel document while it's pending. Consular processing finishes at a U.S. consulate abroad. Adjustment is usually preferable when you're eligible for it; the catch is that eligibility depends on how you entered and your status history, which is exactly what the review determines.

Who is eligible to adjust status inside the U.S.?

The main groups: spouses, parents, and minor children of U.S. citizens who entered lawfully (even after an overstay); family- and employment-based applicants who maintained status with a current priority date; K-1 entrants who married their petitioner; asylees a year after grant; and some people grandfathered under old 245(i) rules despite entry problems. Each group has its own traps — the label matters less than your specific history.

Can I work and travel while my I-485 is pending?

Yes — the package includes the I-765 work permit and I-131 travel document, typically arriving within a few months, and the work permit is valid for any employer. Critical warning: leaving the U.S. without advance parole abandons most pending adjustments (H-1B and L-1 holders traveling on valid visas are the main exception). We file everything together on day one so nothing waits in a second line.

I overstayed my visa. Can I still adjust?

If you entered lawfully and you're the spouse, parent, or unmarried under-21 child of a U.S. citizen — usually yes: the overstay and even unauthorized work are forgiven for immediate relatives. Preference-category and employment applicants face stricter status rules. And if you entered without inspection, adjustment generally isn't available without a special basis — do not file anything before that analysis.

How long does adjustment of status take?

Marriage and family cases at most field offices currently run roughly 9–16 months to the green card, with the work permit arriving well before. Employment cases depend on your priority date and service center. The bigger lever is avoiding the RFE: a complete, consistent package moves through in one pass; a patched one can drag past two years.

What happens at the adjustment interview — and will I even have one?

Marriage cases almost always interview — both spouses, questions about the relationship, originals in hand. Employment and some family cases are increasingly waived into approval without one. If you're called in, preparation is straightforward: know your own file, bring what the notice lists, and stay consistent with everything previously filed — which is easy when the filing was built consistently to begin with.
