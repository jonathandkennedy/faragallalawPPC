How to search for an ICE detainee yourself

1. Try the ICE Online Detainee Locator

Go to locator.ice.gov and search by A-number and country of birth — or by name, date of birth, and country. The A-number is 9 digits printed on almost any immigration paperwork (old notices, work permits, prior applications), and it's the key that unlocks everything. Names must match booking records exactly, so try spelling variations.

2. If they're not listed, don't assume the worst

People booked in the last day or two often don't appear yet, transfers between facilities happen without notice, and some local holds precede ICE custody entirely. Check again after a few hours, try the name variations — and note when and where they were picked up, because that's what a lawyer uses to track them down.

3. Get an attorney on record before the transfer

Once located, the clock matters: detainees are routinely moved to facilities hundreds of miles away, in different courts, harder to visit and defend. Filing the G-28 attorney appearance and starting the bond request before a transfer is the single most time-sensitive move a family can make. That's the part we do.
